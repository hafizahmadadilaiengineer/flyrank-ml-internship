01 · ABSTRACT

The short version

Question: Which content pages should a content team review first when review capacity is limited?

Method: I framed the problem as a ranking and decision-support task and compared a transparent Week-4 baseline with Logistic Regression, Decision Tree, and Random Forest models.

Validation: The models were evaluated with a client-grouped split, and label-derived trend fields were excluded from the predictive feature set to reduce leakage risk.

Result: The Week-4 baseline measured 0.72 Precision@50, while Random Forest measured 0.46 Precision@50 on the same top-50 decision.

Conclusion: For this specific review-prioritization decision, the simpler baseline produced the strongest measured top-50 precision, so the final playbook emphasizes transparent reason codes and human review rather than automatic intervention.

02 · INTRODUCTION

The decision matters more than the model.

Content teams cannot review every page with equal attention. The practical problem is therefore not simply predicting whether a page is declining, but deciding which pages deserve review first.

This project frames content refresh prioritization as a decision-support problem. The output is a ranked queue containing a score, reason code, and suggested action.

The system is designed to help a human reviewer allocate limited editorial time. It does not automatically determine that a page should be refreshed, and it does not claim to predict Google's ranking algorithm.

07 · RANKED RECOMMENDATIONS

A queue for people, not robots.

01

Low CTR visible pages

low_ctr_visible_page

Review visibility, CTR, search intent and SERP context. Use “Review Soon” or “Refresh candidate” only when the human check supports it.

02

Stale visible pages

stale_visible_page

Consider refresh when the content is genuinely outdated and other evidence supports intervention. Update age alone is not enough.

03

General review

general_review

Monitor or investigate when evidence is insufficient for a stronger intervention.

17,756 low-CTR visible pages 12,237 general-review pages 7 stale-visible pages 102 refresh candidates

Human review before action

✓ Check whether the content is actually outdated.
✓ Check search intent and current SERP context.
✓ Check content quality and business relevance.
✓ Record disagreement between the queue and reviewer judgment.
Do not automate: publishing, deletion/pruning, URL changes, canonical changes, merging pages, changing search intent, declaring recovery, or claiming a Google ranking-factor explanation.
08 · DECAY / REFRESH SIGNAL

Age is a clue, not a trigger.

The earlier signal audit showed observed mean trend values of approximately -4.78 for pages updated within 0–180 days, -4.72 for 181–365 days, and -96.17 for 366–730 days.

Important caveat: the 366–730-day bucket contained only 5 pages. The large negative value is therefore directional, not strong evidence of a general content-decay effect.

Practical rule: use update age alongside visibility and engagement signals when prioritizing review; never let age alone trigger an automatic refresh.

09 · LIMITATIONS & HONEST FRAMING

What this study does not prove.

  • The declining label is a proxy derived from an existing trend field, not a separate future outcome.
  • The study is observational and does not establish that refreshing content causes recovery.
  • The baseline winning Precision@50 does not mean it is universally superior to ML models.
  • The experiment does not explain or prove Google's ranking algorithm.
  • The available data cannot capture every editorial, business, or content-quality consideration.
  • The queue is decision support and requires human review.
